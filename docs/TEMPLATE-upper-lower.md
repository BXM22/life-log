# Workout template: Upper/Lower 3-4 day plan

The app ships with Brennen's Upper/Lower 3-4 day plan as its first workout template. The plan says to run one program at a time, so the app keeps one active template and others can be added later. Plan priorities: rear delts, upper chest, upper back, bench press, handstand (push) and muscle-up (pull).

**How the app uses the template**

- Today shows the session for the weekday (Saturday: Upper B) with a Start button. A BJJ class is a simple done check on Monday and Wednesday.
- Warm-up sets and working sets are logged separately. The app suggests the warm-up weight at about 50% of the last working weight (lighter for isolations).
- Each exercise shows its target, such as 3 x 5-8, and its rest time. A rest timer starts when a working set is saved: 3:00 after heavy compounds, 1:30 to 2:00 after isolations.
- Timed holds (handstand, false-grip hang, farmer carry) log seconds. One-arm and per-leg work logs left and right. Pull-ups, pike push-ups and similar log reps with no weight.
- The first exercise of each day shows last week's result and what to beat, because the plan says to add weight or reps every week there.
- Slots with options, such as hack squat, leg press or back squat, remember the chosen option. Swapping hack squat for leg press (the plan's half-marathon note) is one tap.
- Trim a set removes one working set from today's targets without changing history. The plan says to trim, not skip, after hard BJJ.
- Upper, Lower and post-BJJ warm-up checklists show before the first set. The plan's back-training cues are one tap away on each back exercise.
- Personal records rank timed holds by longest time and bodyweight moves by most reps.

**Back-training cues** (shown with each back exercise)

- Every back exercise is the same four movements: at the bottom the shoulder blade is up and forward (lat stretched); start by pulling the shoulder down (vertical pulls) or back (rows); finish with the elbow driving down and slightly in, or straight back on rows; return slowly and let the blade open again.
- One-arm neutral-grip pulldown: best lat isolation. Do not just grab the handle; get the stretch and the shoulder-down start.
- Row, semi-pronated grip: let the blade protract at the bottom, then drive the elbow back. Think of the arms as hooks from the elbow down so the biceps do not turn it into a curl.
- Overhand vertical pull: shoulders down first, elbows down toward the sides, slow lat stretch at the top. For width, stay upright with elbows slightly in front. If step one is hard, do a set of scap pulls only (the warm-up set).
- Intent and progression beat extra volume: 2 hard sets at 5-8 reps, twice a week. Log weights and beat them.
- Rear delts get direct work on upper days. No direct lower back or trap work: RDLs and the Thursday heavy hold cover them.

**Rules from the plan**

- Lifting sets go to or near failure; one warm-up set per exercise.
- Rest 3 min after heavy compounds, 1.5-2 min after isolations.
- Add weight or reps every week on the first exercise of each day.
- Use lifting straps on all pulling.
- Beat up from BJJ? Trim a set or two instead of skipping.

**Notes**

- If Monday's pike push-ups leave the shoulders sore for Tuesday, drop to 2 sets and keep the handstand holds.
- Calisthenics progression: longer handstand holds, then a wall-facing kick-up; 6 strict pull-ups, then a top pause; slow dip negatives, then the transition on a low bar.
- Half-marathon block: swap hack squat for leg press and fit runs on Friday and Sunday.
- The Lower day is once a week (about 15 sets), so keep the 3-minute rests and expect 75-90 minutes.
- Do not run this plan together with the 8-week Low-Volume Hypertrophy block; pick one.
